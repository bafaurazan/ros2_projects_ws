# Mock Viva / Committee Defense

Used by `/viva` (a.k.a. `/defense`). Prepares the candidate for the oral examination — UK/EU/AUS *viva voce* with internal + external examiners, or US committee defense. Same goal: defend the contribution under live, sometimes hostile, questioning.

## Two modes

- **Question-bank mode** — generate the questions likely to come, organized, so the candidate can prepare answers.
- **Live mock mode** — Claude *is* the examiner: ask a question, wait for the real answer, probe the response, escalate. This rehearses the actual pressure. Run one question at a time; follow up on weak answers the way a real examiner pounces on hesitation.

Ask the candidate which mode they want, and what to focus on (whole thesis, one paper, methods, the contribution).

## Core question banks

**The contribution (always opens the viva):**
- In one sentence, what is your original contribution to knowledge?
- Why does it matter, and to whom?
- What is genuinely new here versus what was already known?
- If you removed one paper, would the thesis still stand?

**Coherence (the PhD-by-publication-specific risk):**
- Why these papers? How do they form one program rather than a collection?
- Walk me through the thread connecting them.
- Which paper is weakest, and why is it still in the thesis?

**Methodology:**
- Justify [method]. Why not [obvious alternative]?
- How do your varied methods cohere into one strategy?
- What are the threats to validity, and how did you handle them?

**Critical self-awareness (examiners reward honesty here):**
- What are the limitations of the *whole program*?
- What would you do differently with hindsight?
- What's the strongest objection to your thesis, and what's your answer?

**Positioning & impact:**
- Where does this sit relative to [key author/debate]?
- How would [a named skeptic in the field] attack this?
- What's the next study you'd run?

**Per-paper deep dives:** for each paper — its specific claim, the decision you'd most expect to be challenged, the result you're least sure of.

## Defense strategy to coach

- **Know your contribution cold** — it's the spine of every answer; bridge back to it.
- **Concede gracefully where right.** Examiners test judgment, not infallibility. "That's a fair point; the limitation is X, which is why I bound the claim to Y" beats defensiveness. Knowing what you *don't* claim is strength.
- **Defend where defensible** — with evidence and calm, not volume. Have the one-line rebuttal ready for the obvious attacks.
- **Buy thinking time** — restating the question, "that's an interesting point, let me think," is fine and professional.
- **Separate fatal from survivable.** Most viva questions seek corrections, not failure; don't catastrophize a tough question into a verdict.

## `/elevator` — the contribution pitch ladder

The ability to state the contribution concisely is foundational: it opens the viva, anchors every defense answer, and is required for conferences, three-minute-thesis competitions, networking, and proposal panels. Help the researcher build a **pitch ladder** at three lengths, all expressing the same contribution claim:
- **30 seconds** — one or two sentences: the problem, the contribution, why it matters. The viva opener and the networking line.
- **3 minutes** — the 3MT-style account: problem, gap, what was done across the papers, the finding, and the significance, in accessible language without sacrificing precision.
- **Conference talk / panel** — the extended version with method and evidence.

Coach for clarity over jargon and for a confident, non-hedged statement of the contribution (hedging belongs in the limitations, not the pitch). Derive all three from the roadmap's contribution claim so they remain consistent with the thesis.

## After a mock

Give a debrief: which answers were strong, which exposed a soft spot in the *thesis* (not just the answer — a question you couldn't answer well often signals real work to do), and a short list of contribution-defending lines to drill. If a mock reveals a genuine hole, route it back to `/supervisor` or `/devils-advocate` — better found here than in the room.

# The Synthesis Chapter (Kappa)

Used by `/synthesis`. This is the chapter that turns a stack of papers into a doctorate. Examiners read it first and weight it heavily; it is where the *independent, original contribution* is argued as a whole. In US programs the analogue is the integrative introduction + conclusion that frame the article-based dissertation — same job, bend the framing accordingly.

## What it must do

The papers prove pieces; the synthesis proves there's **one program** and states the contribution that no single paper makes alone. Its job:
- Articulate the **overarching contribution** explicitly — the thing the candidate will defend in the viva.
- Make the **golden thread** visible: why *these* papers, in this order, are one coherent inquiry rather than a portfolio of convenience.
- Show the whole exceeds the sum: the cross-cutting insight that only emerges across papers.
- Demonstrate doctoral-level **critical self-awareness**: methodology coherence, limitations of the program (not just per-paper), and reflexivity.

## Structure (adapt to discipline and system)

1. **Introduction & context** — the problem space, why it matters, and a clear statement of the overarching research question and contribution claim up front. Don't make the examiner hunt for it.
2. **The research program narrative** — the intellectual journey: how the questions evolved, why each paper was the next necessary step. This is a *story of inquiry*, not a list of abstracts.
3. **Conceptual / theoretical framework** — the lens uniting the papers; how it developed across them.
4. **Methodological coherence** — why the chosen methods (often varied across papers) together form a defensible, complementary strategy. Examiners probe this hard in mixed-method portfolios.
5. **Paper-by-paper positioning** — for each: its specific contribution and, crucially, *how it connects to the others and to the whole*. Connections, not summaries.
6. **Integrated discussion** — the synthesized findings: the meta-level claim, what the program collectively establishes, situated in the wider literature.
7. **Original contribution to knowledge** — stated baldly and defensibly. What is now known that wasn't? Why does it matter? This paragraph is the thesis.
8. **Limitations of the program** — honest, whole-program reflection (beyond individual papers' limitations).
9. **Implications & future research** — where this opens doors; theory, practice, method.

## How to coach it

- Start from the **contribution claim** in the roadmap. If the papers don't actually support a single claim, that's a structural problem to surface *now*, not in the viva — route to `/committee` or `/supervisor`.
- Push relentlessly on **connection over summary**. The commonest weak kappa just re-describes each paper. Ask: "what does the reader now understand that they couldn't from any single paper?"
- Keep the candidate's voice and ownership — the synthesis is the most personal, authorial part of the thesis. Scaffold structure and interrogate logic; don't write their argument for them. The integrity stance matters most here, because this chapter *is* the candidate's claim to the doctorate.
- Run an `/integrity-citations` pass and a `/devils-advocate` pass on the draft kappa before any human sees it — it carries the most weight and the most risk.

# Research Craft — Methodology, Theory, Front Matter, Gap, Ethics

Consolidated reference for several focused commands. Each operates within the integrity stance: Claude scaffolds, structures, and interrogates; the substantive scholarly judgments remain the researcher's.

## `/methodology` — design and justify methods

Help the researcher specify and *defend* a methodological approach. A doctoral methods section is judged less on the choice itself than on the quality of its justification.

Work through:
- **Alignment** — does the design follow from the research question and the chosen paradigm? Misalignment between an interpretivist question and a positivist design is a common examiner target.
- **Sampling / case selection** — strategy, size, justification, and limitations. For qualitative work, address saturation or information power rather than statistical power.
- **Instruments / materials** — validity and reliability, or for qualitative work, trustworthiness (credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability).
- **Analysis plan** — specified *before* seeing results where applicable; pre-registration where the field supports it.
- **Threats to validity** — internal, external, construct, statistical-conclusion (quantitative); reflexivity and positionality (qualitative). For each, the mitigation.
- **Coherence across the portfolio** — when papers use different methods, articulate why they are complementary, since the synthesis chapter and viva will both probe this.

Output a structured methods rationale the researcher can adapt; never assert that a design "is" rigorous without the reasoning that makes it so.

## `/theory` — develop or critique the theoretical framework

The framework is the lens that converts data into a contribution. Help the researcher:
- **Articulate the framework** — its core constructs, their relationships, and its assumptions.
- **Justify its selection** over rival framings; engage the alternatives rather than ignoring them.
- **Define boundary conditions** — where the framework applies and where it does not. Vague boundaries are a frequent reviewer objection.
- **Trace its development across the papers** — for the synthesis chapter, show how the framework evolved.
- **Stress-test for novelty** — distinguish a genuinely new conceptual contribution from the relabelling of existing constructs. The Skeptic persona (`/committee`) is well suited to this test.

## `/abstract` — title, abstract, keywords

These determine whether a paper is read or desk-rejected, and they are read by examiners first; treat them as high-leverage.
- **Title** — precise, informative, and discoverable; conveys the contribution, not merely the topic.
- **Abstract** — follows the paper's logic in miniature (for empirical work: background, objective, method, key results with specifics, conclusion/implication). Quantified results where available; no claim absent from the paper.
- **Keywords** — chosen for discoverability in the field's indexing conventions, complementing rather than repeating the title.
Draft from the researcher's actual content and results only; mark any figure requiring their verification.

## `/gap` — gap analysis and novelty check

Establish that the contribution is genuinely new and the gap genuinely real — the question on which originality turns.
- Survey what is established, using real web search; paraphrase and cite, and do not invent sources (see `references/integrity-and-citations.md`).
- Locate the gap precisely: is it empirical, theoretical, methodological, or contextual? "No one has studied X in context Y" is the weakest form; press toward a gap that matters.
- Test the claim of novelty against the closest existing work, candidly. If the gap is smaller than the researcher believes, say so now rather than letting an examiner say it later.
- Frame the gap as a motivated problem, not an absence — why its resolution advances the field.

## `/ethics` — research ethics and data management

Support the procedural and ethical scaffolding a doctorate requires.
- **Approval** — institutional ethics / IRB / HREC requirements, informed consent, and the elements a committee expects in an application.
- **Risk and vulnerability** — participant welfare, vulnerable populations, deception and debriefing where relevant.
- **Data management** — storage, anonymization/pseudonymization, retention, sharing and FAIR principles, and any regulatory regime the researcher identifies (e.g. data-protection law in their jurisdiction).
- **Research integrity** — authorship and contribution norms (e.g. the CRediT taxonomy), conflicts of interest, and responsible conduct.
Claude provides structure and prompts; it does not certify compliance, and should direct the researcher to their institution's ethics body for binding determinations.

## `/openscience` — pre-registration, reproducibility, and open research

Open and reproducible practice is increasingly expected and strengthens a thesis against the Skeptic and the examiner. Help the researcher adopt what fits their field:
- **Pre-registration** — record hypotheses, design, and analysis plan before data collection (e.g. on OSF or AsPredicted), which converts exploratory work into confirmatory and forecloses accusations of analytic flexibility.
- **Registered reports** — where the field offers them, in-principle acceptance based on the question and method *before* results, which immunizes against publication bias and is excellent for a portfolio.
- **Open data, code, and materials** — sharing under FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable), with appropriate anonymization and licensing; deposit in a recognized repository with a persistent identifier.
- **Reproducibility** — analysis that another researcher could rerun: documented code, computational environment, and a clear data-to-result trail. An examiner may ask whether the analysis is reproducible.
- **Transparency in reporting** — field-appropriate reporting standards (e.g. CONSORT, PRISMA, or qualitative reporting guidelines) so methods and results are complete.

Match the level of openness to the discipline, the ethics constraints on the data, and the institution's expectations; Claude advises and structures, and defers to the researcher's ethics approval on what may be shared.

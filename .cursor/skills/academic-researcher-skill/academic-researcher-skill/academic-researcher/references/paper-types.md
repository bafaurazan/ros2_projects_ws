# Paper Types, Drafting, Editing & Journal Fit

Used by `/draft`, `/edit`, and `/journal`. Pick the structure that matches the paper's logic, not a default.

## Integrity reminder for `/draft`

Draft depth is whatever the user asks for in the moment — outline, zero-draft, or full prose. In all cases:
- Work only from the researcher's **actual** data, results, and analytic decisions. If results aren't supplied, scaffold with explicit `[YOUR RESULT HERE — e.g. mean, CI, p]` placeholders; never invent numbers, findings, or sources.
- The **argument and interpretation are the researcher's**. When drafting a discussion or conclusion, surface options and ask which they actually believe rather than manufacturing a stance.
- Mark anything generated that needs the researcher's verification. A draft is a starting block, not a finish line they sign blind.

## Empirical paper — IMRaD

The default for quantitative/empirical work. The logic is an hourglass: broad → narrow → broad.

- **Introduction** — funnel from field-level importance to the specific gap to *this* study's question/hypotheses. End on what you did and why it matters. ~10–15% of length.
- **Methods** — enough for replication: design, participants/sample, materials/measures, procedure, analysis plan. Past tense, no results. Pre-registration / ethics noted here.
- **Results** — findings only, no interpretation. Lead with the answer to the main question; tables/figures do the heavy lifting, text highlights not narrates them.
- **and** — (the small "a").
- **Discussion** — interpret against the question and the literature, then limitations, then implications, then a contribution-anchored conclusion. Mirror the intro funnel in reverse.

Reviewer hot spots: under-justified analysis choices, results that don't map to stated hypotheses, discussion that over-claims beyond the data, missing limitations/reflexivity.

## Systematic / PRISMA review

A contribution in itself and an excellent Paper 1. Structure:
- Rationale + question (often PICO/SPIDER-framed) → **protocol** (ideally pre-registered, e.g. PROSPERO) → search strategy → screening → extraction → synthesis (narrative or meta-analytic) → quality/risk-of-bias appraisal → discussion.
- Must include a **PRISMA flow diagram** with counts at each stage. See `references/integrity-and-citations.md` for the protocol Claude follows when actually running or auditing one.

## Theoretical / conceptual paper

No new data; the contribution is a new framework, model, typology, or argument.
- Structure follows the *argument's* logic, not IMRaD: problem with current thinking → the move you make → development of the framework → boundary conditions → implications → research agenda.
- Reviewer hot spots: "what's actually new here vs. relabeling?", insufficient engagement with rival framings, hand-wavy boundary conditions. The Skeptic persona (`/committee`) is invaluable on these.

## Methods paper

Introduces/validates a method, instrument, or analytic approach.
- Structure: the problem existing methods can't solve → the proposed method → validation/benchmarking → worked example → limitations & scope of applicability → availability (code/data).

## Scoping review

Maps the breadth of a field/question rather than synthesizing effect sizes — good for staking out a young area. Follows PRISMA-ScR; lighter on appraisal, heavier on charting the landscape and naming gaps.

## Editing (`/edit`) — what good academic editing changes (and what it must not)

Improve the prose **without changing the researcher's claims or strength of claim**. Targets:
- **Register**: formal but not bloated; cut nominalizations ("conducted an investigation of" → "investigated"), prefer active where the field allows.
- **Signposting**: each paragraph one idea, topic sentence first; explicit transitions; "roadmap" sentences at section starts.
- **Hedging calibration**: claims matched to evidence — neither "proves" nor timid mush. Watch this closely; mis-hedging is a claim change, so flag rather than silently alter.
- **Concision**: kill throat-clearing ("It is important to note that"), redundancy, and zombie nouns.
- **Paragraph architecture**: point → evidence → explanation → link.
- **Consistency**: terminology, tense conventions per section, citation style.
Always preserve meaning; if a fix would alter a claim, mark it as a query, don't impose it.

## Journal fit (`/journal`)

Selecting the target venue *before* drafting shapes scope, length, and framing. Assess:
- **Scope match** — does the journal publish this question/method? (Read recent issues; desk-reject risk lives here.)
- **Audience** — who you're writing for changes the intro framing and what's assumed.
- **Method fit** — some venues won't touch certain designs; some demand pre-registration.
- **Ambition vs. realism** — a venue ladder: a reach, a solid target, a safe fallback. Map turnaround times against the milestone schedule — a slow prestigious journal can cost a year.
- **Predatory-journal screen** — flag pay-to-publish-with-no-real-review venues; check against recognized indexes and the Skeptic's nose. Never steer a candidate toward a venue that would taint the thesis.
Use real web search to check current scope, recent articles, and turnaround reputation; cite what you find; don't assert impact factors or acceptance rates from memory.

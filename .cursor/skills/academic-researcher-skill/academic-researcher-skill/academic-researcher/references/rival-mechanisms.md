# The Rival-Mechanism Audit

Reference for `/rivals`. Where `/gap` asks *"is the contribution new?"*, `/rivals` asks the harder question every examiner and Reviewer 2 will ask: **"even if your result comes out exactly as predicted, how many *other* theories predict the same result without your mechanism?"** A headline finding that six rival accounts also predict is not evidence for your account — it is evidence for the *disjunction* of all seven.

This audit was developed on a real experimental design where a systematic hunt found **seven** rival theories, each predicting the study's key interaction through a different mechanism — a discovery that reshaped the design before a single participant was recruited. That is the audit working as intended: rivals found at design time cost a condition; rivals found at review time cost the paper.

## The procedure

### 1. State the claim precisely

Write the prediction as *result + mechanism*: "X will vary with Y **because** [mechanism M]." The audit targets the *because*.

### 2. Enumerate the rivals exhaustively

For the predicted result, list every theoretical account that would predict the **same observable pattern** through a **different mechanism**. Search deliberately, not just from memory:
- Adjacent theories in the same subfield (the obvious competitors).
- Theories from *neighboring* fields that touch the same variables (attention, motivation, measurement artifacts, demand characteristics, practice/fatigue, individual-difference confounds).
- Deflationary accounts: "the effect is real but boring" explanations (floor/ceiling effects, scale artifacts, regression to the mean, selection).

For each rival, record: **the rival's own mechanism**, stated in its own vocabulary and fairly (steelmanned, not straw-manned); **what its own literature actually says** — verified by reading or searching, cited, and never invented; and **where it makes the same prediction as yours, and where (if anywhere) it diverges**.

### 3. Design the discharge

For each rival, specify the concrete design element that would *discriminate* between it and your mechanism:
- a condition, manipulation, or population where the two accounts predict **different** outcomes;
- a measure that indexes your mechanism but not the rival's (or vice versa);
- a boundary condition your account predicts and the rival does not.

One well-chosen condition can discharge several rivals at once — hunt for these high-leverage discriminators before adding cells. A rival that *cannot* be discharged by any feasible design must be acknowledged in the paper as a live alternative, honestly.

### 4. Audit the gap claim itself against primary sources

The most dangerous rival is the one that says **your gap does not exist**. Before staking a claim that "theory T lacks X" or "no account explains Y":
- Read (or have the researcher read) the **primary sources**, including recent editions and the theory's own retrospectives — not just secondary summaries. Gaps frequently collapse under full reads: the parameter exists but under different vocabulary, or the claim needs re-scoping from "T has no X" to "T has X but never *derives* anything from it."
- When a gap claim survives, state it in its **narrowest defensible form**, restricted to the works actually examined — never as an unrestricted absence claim about an entire literature.
- When a gap claim dies, **retract it visibly and re-scope**: the corrected, narrower gap is usually more interesting and far more defensible than the dead broad one.

### 5. Produce the rivals table

Deliver the audit as a table the researcher keeps with the protocol:

| # | Rival account | Its mechanism | Predicts our result because… | Diverges from us at… | Discharged by… | Status |
|---|---------------|---------------|------------------------------|----------------------|----------------|--------|

Statuses: **DISCHARGED (by design)** / **DISCHARGED (by measure)** / **LIVE — acknowledged in paper** / **LIVE — design change needed**.

## Conduct

- **Steelman every rival.** The audit only protects the researcher if the rivals are presented at full strength; a weak rival discharged is no protection at all.
- **Verify, don't assume.** Claims about what a rival theory predicts must be checked against its literature (use real search; cite; flag the unverifiable). Never invent a rival position — misdescribing a competitor is as fatal in review as ignoring one.
- **Feed the results forward.** Discharge conditions go into `/methodology`; live rivals go into the paper's limitations and the `/viva` question bank; re-scoped gaps update `/gap` and the roadmap's contribution claim.
